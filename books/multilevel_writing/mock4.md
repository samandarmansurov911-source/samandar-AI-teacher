# MOCK 4 — Public Library

## Topshiriqlar

**Vaziyat:** Siz shahringizdagi ommaviy kutubxona a'zosisiz. Sizga quyidagi xabar keldi:

> Dear Library Member,
> We are updating our services and would like your input. What kind of books or materials should we add? Do you think we should also organize workshops or reading clubs? Please share your thoughts.
> Sincerely,
> Library Coordinator

**Task 1.1 – Informal Letter.** Write to a friend who also uses the library. Share your opinion and ideas about the changes. *Write about 50 words.*

**Task 1.2 – Formal Letter.** Write to the library coordinator. Suggest improvements and give reasons for your suggestions. *Write 120–150 words.*

**Task 2 – Forum Post.** Online discussion: *"Should schools focus more on practical skills (cooking, finance, first aid) than academic subjects?"* Give your opinion and explain with examples. *Write 180–200 words.*

### Task 1.2 – Formal Letter

Dear Library Coordinator,

Thank you for reaching out to library members for suggestions. I truly appreciate the opportunity to share my thoughts.

Firstly, I believe the library should expand its collection of audiobooks and e-books, as many users now prefer digital formats for convenience. Adding more contemporary fiction, especially by diverse authors, would also attract younger readers. Including more academic materials, such as test preparation books and subject-specific references, would be helpful for students.

As for new services, organizing weekend reading clubs or monthly workshops on writing, public speaking, or digital literacy could greatly benefit the community. These events would not only promote a love of reading but also help people connect and learn new skills.

Thank you again for considering members' opinions. I hope my suggestions are useful and help improve our already wonderful library.

Sincerely,
Lena Roberts

### Task 2 – Forum Post

In my opinion, schools should strike a balance between academic subjects and practical skills, but there's a strong case for giving more emphasis to practical skills like cooking, finance, and first aid. These are essential for everyday life and personal development.

For example, learning basic financial literacy—like budgeting, saving, and understanding credit—could prevent young adults from making costly mistakes later on. I know someone who struggled with debt after college because they never learned how to manage money effectively. If schools taught these skills, it would set students up for a more stable future.

Cooking is another vital skill that promotes independence and healthy living. Knowing how to prepare nutritious meals instead of relying on fast food can have long-term health benefits, as I've seen with friends who've adopted healthier habits after learning to cook in a home economics class.

First aid is also crucial. In emergencies, knowing how to respond can save lives. Schools already teach subjects like math and science, but practical skills like these would equip students with tools they'll use every day.

In conclusion, while academic knowledge is important, integrating more practical skills into the curriculum would better prepare students for real-world challenges and contribute to their well-being.
